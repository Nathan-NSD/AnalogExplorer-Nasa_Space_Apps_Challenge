# AnalogExplorer field atlas

Where on Earth to rehearse the Moon and Mars, and what Earth cannot teach.

Mission profile: **Habitat / Construction**. Data: real, 23,039 land tiles of Earth compared with 7 lunar and Martian targets. This document is generated by rules from the dataset (no language model writes it): `python -m src.report --atlas`.

## How to read this atlas

- An **analog** is a place on Earth that reproduces some conditions of a lunar or Martian site. No place reproduces them all, so each site comes with what matches and what does not.
- **Closer than X % of Earth pairs**: the site is more similar to the target than X % of random pairs of places on Earth are to each other. 50 % means 'as alike as two ordinary places on Earth'. For one variable, pairs identical on it (e.g. two places without any bare ground) are left out, and 50 % always means a gap of one tolerance, the unit of the score.
- **Relative score**: 1 for the best Earth tile for this target and mission.
- Coordinates are tile centres; a tile is about 110 km wide (narrower in longitude near the poles). A site is named after the country and the physical region of its tile centre (Natural Earth; a reading aid, outside the score). Every share of Earth ('closer than X % of Earth pairs', 'top X %') is a share of land area: a tile counts for its land surface, so the many narrow tiles near the poles do not weigh more than their area.
- Evaluated here: topography, temperature, water and ice, illumination (with the height of the Sun), bare ground. Not evaluated: geology and mineralogy (only the share of bare ground is compared); dust, wind and aeolian processes; caves and subsurface environments (case studies only, for the Mare Tranquillitatis pit); biology and habitability.

## What Earth can never reproduce

These limits hold for every site, whatever its score:

| Factor | Moon | Mars | Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gravity | About 1/6 g | About 0.38 g | Mobility, drilling and fluid tests are not representative. |
| Atmosphere | Near vacuum | Very thin (about 0.6 kPa) | Heat transfer, wind and dust behave differently. |
| Radiation | No atmosphere, no global field | Thin atmosphere, no global field | Surface dose is higher than on Earth. |
| Regolith / dust | Abrasive, electrostatically charged | Fine, specific composition | Simulants are needed; the analogy is partial. |

## How the ranking works, in plain words

- Earth's land is cut into tiles of 1 x 1 degree (about 110 km). Each tile and each target is described, for every variable, by the distribution of its values, not by an average.
- A tile is compared with a target variable by variable (distance between the two distributions). The gap is judged against a yardstick: how different two places on Earth picked at random are from each other.
- The score is a weighted geometric mean, so a very poor match on one variable cannot be hidden by good matches elsewhere. The weights come from the mission profile. The penalty grows with the square of the gap for close matches and linearly for distant ones, so one very poor variable does not decide the order alone.
- A variable that no place on Earth can match (for example the lunar day/night temperature swing) leaves the score and is reported with figures instead of quietly deciding the ranking.
- Neighbouring tiles look alike, so the list keeps distinct sites only (at least 300 km apart).
- Under-studied means at least 500 km from every analog of our sourced catalogue and outside Antarctica, the Atacama, Iceland and Hawaii, which the challenge names as already well studied.
- Shares of Earth are shares of land area, not counts of tiles: a pair of 'random places on Earth' is two points drawn on land, and 'top 1 %' is the best 1 % of the land surface.
- The credibility check asks whether analog sites known from the literature rank better than random places on land on the property they are known for. Its protocol was fixed before looking at the result; it was revised once afterwards (ranks as shares of land area, tied places sharing their average rank), and both sets of results are kept by the team. Aridity, ground below 0 C and bare ground are set to 1 by physics for every target, so this check mainly shows that dry, cold, bare and rough places rank above random land; it does not show that the ranking tells one target from another (see the cross-check with the other body). The p-value is also given after a Holm correction for testing every target.
- Score and status use one yardstick, the tolerance: for a variable where many pairs of places are identical (bare ground, ground below 0 C), the site's gap is scaled by the share of pairs that differ, so that 50 % means 'one tolerance away' for every variable.
- A status reads 'closer than X % of Earth pairs' among the pairs that differ on the variable: two places that both have no bare ground at all, or are both frozen all year, say nothing about how close a match is.

## Data and references

- Earth topography: Copernicus DEM GLO-90 (ESA), AWS open data
- Earth climate: NASA POWER climatology (MERRA-2, CERES SYN1deg), AWS open data
- Earth bare ground: ESA WorldCover 10 m 2021 north of 60 S; SCAR Antarctic Digital Database rock outcrop v7.11 south of it (both CC BY 4.0)
- Moon: NASA PDS: LRO LOLA, LRO Diviner; NASA PGDA illumination
- Mars: NASA PDS: MGS MOLA; Mars Climate Database v6.2 (LMD, with ESA and CNES)
- Earth tiles of 1 x 1 deg, weighted by their land area; topography at a common ~463 m pixel on every world; Earth climate on the NASA POWER grids (0.5 x 0.625 deg and 1 deg), 12 months.
- Known analogs: sourced catalogue of the project (Preston & Dartnell 2014, Foucher et al. 2021, NASA Artemis and ESA PANGAEA training sites), used for the credibility check and the under-studied flag.
- Mission weights and thresholds are hypotheses to calibrate; the interactive tool shows how stable each result is when they change.
